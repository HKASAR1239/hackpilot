# Pilot proposal

## 1. Pilot clear signs plus an optional welcome

- Known: one entrance, two staff; confusion about bookings, walk-in tickets and access assistance.
- Proposal: movable destination signs and a brief welcome at the shared entrance.
- Hypothesis: signs clarify routine steps; personal guidance resolves exceptions.
- Signage alone uses less staff attention. Add greeting only if it improves clarity without compromising coverage.

Recommend a limited, reversible pilot for arriving visitors and the two arrival staff. Preserve the existing entrance and ticketing process. The combined approach may resolve uncertainty before visitors reach the wrong service point, but that causal mechanism is a hypothesis, not a demonstrated result. Permanent adoption is not yet justified.

The comparator uses the same signs while staff retain existing duties and assistance. Signage alone requires little ongoing attention but cannot resolve every unusual ticket situation or communication need. The combined condition adds proactive greeting when capacity permits. Retain signage alone as the fallback and prefer it if greeting offers no observable advantage or repeatedly disrupts coverage. Verbal orientation alone leaves visitors without guidance when both staff are occupied.

S1 supplies the entrance, staffing and reported-confusion facts. It supplies no queue counts, service times, budget or conversion data. Its explicit restriction is: “Do not invent measured gains or require a new app.” No improvement or verified cost is claimed; the proposal requires no app, additional staff or integration.

The unchanged judging criteria shape this proposal: visitor experience, 40%, through understandable next steps and inclusive help; operational feasibility, 35%, through existing processes, staff coverage and reversibility; clarity of evidence, 25%, through explicit assumptions and prospective observation. These are evidence priorities, not predicted scores.

## 2. One shared arrival point; essential duties come first

- Shared arrival → signs or personal help → existing Entry checks or Tickets.
- Staff 1: entrance checks; greet when free. Staff 2: walk-in ticketing.
- Both offer assistance within their remit. No separate accessibility queue or compulsory greeting.
- Before launch: check layout, access, duties and costs. Suspend greeting when coverage is threatened.

Use movable signs and existing materials where available. Suggested wording: “Already booked? Have your booking ready for Entry checks. Need a ticket? Go to Tickets. Need help with entry or access? Ask either member of staff.” Match labels to actual service locations and add arrows only after checking the layout. Position signs before the choice point, outside circulation and emergency access paths. Use plain language, readable high-contrast text and visual cues that do not rely on colour alone.

Staff 1 retains entrance and checking duties and can say: “Welcome. Do you already have a booking, or do you need a ticket? I can also help with entry or access.” Visitors who know their destination can proceed without stopping. Staff 2 handles walk-in transactions through the existing process. Both assist within their competence and current responsibilities.

Offer help throughout the shared journey, including to visitors who cannot perceive or understand signs. Ask what would help, respect communication preferences and do not require public disclosure of a disability. Offer a discreet conversation where practical while maintaining essential coverage; use existing assistance arrangements when needed. A shared route alone does not establish accessibility.

Before implementation, responsible museum staff should confirm sightlines, reach, movement and emergency access; staff authority; reusable materials; actual incremental costs; and an acceptable spending limit. Propose a tabletop walkthrough of simultaneous prebooked arrival, a walk-in transaction and an assistance request. Check whether a minimal paper tally fits around service. These checks have not been performed.

Unsupported assumptions: the layout accommodates the setup; the proposed roles preserve coverage; materials are available at acceptable cost; information and assistance meet varied access needs; and observation is feasible without extra staff. If transactions or assistance consume capacity, suspend proactive greeting. If this is routine, prefer signage alone.

## 3. Compare confusion; stop if access or coverage fails

- Proposed trial: two comparable opening days; quieter and busier observation windows.
- Success: lower misdirection and repeat-clarification rates, with access and essential coverage maintained.
- Either staff member stops for blocked access, an unmet access need, uncovered duties or unsafe crowding.
- No results yet. No added benefit: signage alone. Favourable evidence: further trial. Weak evidence: inconclusive.

Proposed duration, not a source fact: observe one quieter and one busier 30-minute window on each of two comparable opening days. On day one, use signage alone in the quieter window and signs plus greeting in the busier window; reverse the allocation on day two. This exposes both conditions to both demand contexts while limiting initial recording burden. Match clock times where practical and keep signs unchanged. Two days offer an initial operational comparison, not a guaranteed adequate sample.

Record arriving visitors, visitors misdirected and visitors seeking repeat clarification. Misdirection means reaching the wrong service point and needing redirection. Repeat clarification means asking again after initial guidance. Count each visitor at most once within each measure per window; a visitor may appear in both measures, so do not add them together. An initial assistance request is not itself confusion.

For fully observed windows, divide each confusion count by arriving visitors and show counts alongside rates. Leave rates undefined when there are no arrivals. Record dates, window times, conditions, visitor mix, group arrivals, crowding, access barriers or voluntary feedback, essential-duty gaps, greeting suspensions and missing observation periods. Missing observations are not zero; do not use a full-window denominator for partially observed events. Recording must not displace essential service, and no extra observer is assumed.

Provisional success requires lower rates for both confusion measures across comparable quieter and busier windows, without observed deterioration in access or essential-duty coverage. Consider feedback and greeting suspensions alongside those rates. Zero confusion in both conditions shows no demonstrated incremental benefit. Sparse arrivals, incomplete records, mixed findings or materially different conditions remain inconclusive. This protocol supports no claim of shorter queues, improved throughput or causal validation.

Either staff member can call an immediate pause for obstructed passage or emergency access, an access need the arrangement cannot accommodate, an uncovered essential duty, or crowding staff cannot safely manage. Suspend greeting, remove or reposition movable pilot items as needed, and restore the existing safe arrangement while addressing the visitor’s immediate need through existing assistance procedures. Retain signs only where safe.

After the trial, no observable incremental benefit or repeated coverage disruption favours signage alone. Favourable evidence supports consideration of another limited trial, not permanent adoption. All field observation and validation remain prospective.
