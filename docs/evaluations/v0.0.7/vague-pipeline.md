# Starting recommendation

## Recommendation

Start with a temporary paper checkout sheet beside a small subset of the club’s equipment, provided those items share a storage point. It would serve members borrowing equipment and whoever handles returns. The working hypothesis is that uncertainty about availability and responsibility makes sharing harder: a visible record could reduce repeated questions and clarify expected returns. This is a proposed starting point, not a demonstrated improvement.

## Proposed routine

Use one row per item, recording its identifier, status, borrower identifier and expected return. At pickup, the borrower checks that the item is present and updates the row before leaving. At return, they put the item back, mark it available and clear the borrower and expected-return fields. Use a club-recognized identifier rather than personal contact details.

If an item is checked out, treat its return time as an expectation, not a guarantee; choose another available item or follow the club’s usual contact route. If an entry is overdue or contradicts what is physically present, mark the status uncertain. A willing club member checks with the borrower or locates the item before correcting the entry. Do not promise availability while status is uncertain.

Illustrative entry only: Tripod T1 | Checked out | Member M07 | Friday, 17:00. After its return: Tripod T1 | Available | — | —.

## Unknowns and alternatives

S1 supplies the goal but no interview results, budget figures or official judging criteria. The current borrowing workflow, storage arrangement, demand and administrative capacity are unknown. The proposal assumes that information gaps matter, members can update paper at storage, someone can resolve discrepancies and enough comparable borrowing attempts occur to learn from.

Before trying it, establish where the selected items are stored and whether someone is willing to reconcile entries. If equipment is dispersed or handoffs are the main problem, route requests through one coordinator using the existing communication channel, subject to their capacity. If checking availability before travelling matters most, consider a shared digital table, subject to access and reliable updates. Paper is the initial choice because it requires no accounts or integration and can be removed easily.

## Reversible learning plan

Observe the next few borrowing attempts under the usual routine, then a similar set using the sheet for the selected equipment. Let actual borrowing activity determine the observation period. For each attempt, note the item and circumstances, unresolved questions, obstacles such as access or supply, status discrepancies, and effort spent updating or reconciling records. Ask whether the member could determine availability and complete the handoff.

Continue provisionally only if comparable attempts suggest fewer unresolved availability or responsibility questions, entries remain dependable and participants find the upkeep manageable. Revise or switch approaches if remote visibility or handoffs dominate. Treat sparse or dissimilar observations as inconclusive. Remove the sheet if it misleads members or adds excessive work; first transfer outstanding loans to the usual routine, then discard the temporary borrower records. All observations and comparisons remain future work.
